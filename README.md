## The Rouge Pur Couture family, and why three lipsticks share one name

YSL's main lipstick line is Rouge Pur Couture, and it's split into three finishes that behave quite differently on the lips. The packaging is what trips people up: same square gold-tube aesthetic, different performance.

| Rouge Pur Couture Satin (Caring Satin with Ceramides) | Satin / creamy | 0.13 oz | US$48 | Everyday wear, comfortable, medium opacity |
| Rouge Pur Couture The Slim | Matte, slim bullet | Standard slim format | US$48 | Precise lining, matte finish, less product per stroke |
| Rouge Pur Couture The Bold | High-shine bold colour | 3 g / 0.11 oz | US$48 | One-stroke intensity with shine, statement wear |

The prices above are what Macy's lists for the Satin and The Slim variants, and what both Macy's and Bloomingdale's list for The Bold. Bloomingdale's has run The Bold at US$40.80 during a 15% off promotion, which is the pattern you should expect from department stores: a short window, then back to full price.

YSL's own product pages describe The Bold in fairly specific terms. The brand claims 5 hours of shine and colour wear, a 10-hour moisturised comfort feel, and a formula built with 40% care ingredients, including red floral oil from the YSL Beauty Ourika Community Gardens and grape seed oil. The performance figures come from a consumer test on 125 women, so treat them as the brand's own numbers rather than independent lab results.

Worth knowing before you decide between them: The Bold is 3 g. The Satin is 0.13 oz, which is roughly 3.7 g. If you're comparing per-gram value across the three, the matte and bold formulations are not automatically the better deal just because the marketing is louder.

## What ysl lipstick costs outside the US

Prices move a lot by market, and this is where people get annoyed.

Canada lists Rouge Pur Couture The Bold at C$56, with YSL Beauty's own site having shown it at C$44.80 during a promotion. Australia lists it at A$72 at Sephora and David Jones. That A$72 works out to roughly US$47, so the "cheaper abroad" theory doesn't hold up on this product. What does make a difference is duty-free and travel retail: one duty-free retailer has listed Rouge Pur Couture in 01 Le Rouge at US$41, which is a real saving against the US$48 counter price.

Price comparison sites show the spread more dramatically. One Australian tracker has logged The Bold in shade 14 Nude Look as low as A$25.40, about two-thirds below retail, at a beauty discounter. That listing was showing out of stock when the data was captured, which tells you the useful part of the story: the deep discounts exist, they last a few days, and they disappear.

So the realistic options are:

- **Department stores on a 15% off event.** Predictable, but you have to wait for the window.
- **Duty-free and travel retail.** Consistently below counter price on core shades.
- **Beauty discounters.** Cheapest when they have stock, unreliable on shade availability.
- **Cashback on top of whatever price you already found.** The only option that stacks with all three above.

That last one is worth a proper look, because it's the part most people skip.
